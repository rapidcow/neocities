              >>> About rapidcow@neocities! <<<

Who are you?  I am rapidcow! (that's obvious lol 6_6),
also known as ethan or yizheng.  I'm a big sucker for
old/low tech, the old web, and free software.  I also
technically own the @rapidcow handle on YouTube, Tumblr;
and the funny .org domain, but I don't do a lot there
now given my love-hate relationship with the Internet?

I don't feel strongly about anonymity (I find it too
inconvenient for to have to maintain a private profile),
though I am obsessed with decentralized and distributed
systems.  The software I write aims to work without an
active Internet connection; my web sites can be pulled
out of the Internet whenever I want.  I also gravitate
towards file formats can only be opened by a specific
program... such as *text files*. ;)  My second favorite
to text files would be Git --- less about the software
but more about the agnostic simplistic object model....

What is this?  My Neocities!  It's sort of an experiment
of replicating my favorite interface (this specifically
mimics the Linux virtual terminal on my Thinkpad, but
I like Unix terminals in general. :)  Also I thought I
wanted this instead of giving out my ``socials.''  (You
know the dread I have when I go to somebody's web site
and it's just a link dump to their accounts on company-
owned platforms elsewhere... wait what do you mean they
specifically have a service for just that?  wut. o_o)

Anyways, I feel an obligation to clarify what I mean by
that --- because thus far it sounds like I wanted us to
self-host everything.  Which... I mean, self-hosting is
cool: I can self-host Mastodon (I in fact have.)  But
that doesn't change the fundamental issue which I like
to call the form of presentation.  On Geocities you are
the host of a cool show; artist of a free open canvas;
creator of a limitless world.  On Twitter and (modern)
YouTube, you are a manufacturer in a colorless factory,
churning out consumable ``content'' for attention and
profits.  You as a person with all the interests that
could sprawl across an entire table are reduced to a
neatly packaged can of avatar, handle, bio, and your
``user-created'' content in a rancid sewer of for-you
recommendations; where human expression is exchanged
as a commodity; and our being compressed into a name
card.  A Fediverse instance implementing Twitter-like
profiles reclaims the control but does not address the
expressive form, while Geocities, despite centralized
and does not grant you control, gives you the range of
expression that characterized the starry old web.

And even if this isn't and might never be anything
like that, I'm glad to at least talk about it.
