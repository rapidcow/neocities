            === ON INFORMATION, TRUST, AND TECHNOLOGY ===
                    February 12, 2025 | rapidcow                  

   Over the last few days I have been thinking about the way my
   university operates -- that somehow, just about in every way,
   technology was invented to control us, the students.  I find
   a twisted sense of amusement at the thought of a course that,
   in desperate hopes of soliciting student attendance, hides
   its quizzes (whose answers are solely imparted during lecture)
   after you have taken them:  It couldn't trust its students to
   use the knowledge for the sheer cause of curiosity over the
   fear of using that power to help others cheat the quizzes and
   pass the course without actually attending.

   I thought, as I walked out of the class room, the names of
   those pieces that I didn't write down.  The fluttering snow
   flew by as the face-numbing wind blew coldly.  For long I've
   been a witness of the passage of time and the bits and pieces
   it blew away.  Like the hundreds upon thousands of snowflakes,
   darting into the horizon, blending with the landscape and
   never to be seen again.  It's not like listening to those
   pieces changed my life so much so that I *had* to find out
   what their names were; yet somehow, the thought that this was
   made inaccessible by deliberate design was enough to upset me.

   Life at school for me has somehow always been this eternal
   struggle with the system: to smuggle out data from their omni-
   scient system; to find ways to break out and project my voice,
   against the things they want me to say and the person they
   want me to be.  The very foundation of every interaction is
   built upon this thin veil of deception and hospitality, and
   --- most importantly --- a total sense of mutual distrust.
   Naturally, the technology that they choose to employ in this
   system serves to perpetuate this: it treats its users with a
   snobbish kind of contempt.
